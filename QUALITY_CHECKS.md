# Motion quality checks from earlier revisions

1. Establish native resolution, frame rate and exact source cut frames. An export size is not proof of source detail.
2. Inspect both sides of every cut. A rejected shot can survive for just three frames; sampled contact sheets alone can miss it.
3. Preserve the user-approved pacing. Long static holds can feel like slow motion even at 1×. Do not replay or stretch footage to fill duration.
4. Distinguish genuine angles from synthetic zooms. Keep the intended narrative and screen direction; do not reintroduce rejected scenes.
5. Check every visible face, hand, garment and small product mark at native size. Generated logos/numbers may be malformed even when the render passes.
6. Check brand spelling and CTA consistency in every ending, including the last scene. If a flash-to-photo treatment is requested, apply it consistently to the actual closing pose.
7. Grade shots coherently while protecting skin and product colors. Avoid arbitrary filters that make garments or copy unreadable.
8. Place typography around faces/products separately for 9:16, 4:5 and 16:9. Check long text, approved font loading, contrast and safe margins.
9. Decode the whole export, then play it at 1× with sound. Stills and silent playback cannot certify motion continuity or an audio mix. Report unreviewed audio explicitly.
10. Keep source, edit decision list and known limitations. A review export is not proof of product fidelity, commercial rights, approval to publish or campaign performance.
